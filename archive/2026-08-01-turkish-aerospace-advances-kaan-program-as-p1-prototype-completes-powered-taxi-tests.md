Turkish Aerospace Industries (TUSAŞ) has released new footage showing the KAAN P1 fighter prototype successfully completing powered taxi tests at its facilities in Ankara. The milestone marks the aircraft’s formal transition into intensive ground testing ahead of its first flight, targeted for later this year.

The low-speed taxi runs, conducted under the aircraft’s own engine power, allow engineers to evaluate braking, steering, engine response, and overall ground handling, standard prerequisites before any prototype is cleared for flight. The P1 is the second prototype of Türkiye’s indigenous fifth-generation fighter and the first built to more closely represent the intended production configuration.

According to program updates, TUSAŞ aims to have the P1 airborne within the next couple of months. Both the P1 and P2 prototypes are expected to be flying by the end of 2026, accelerating the flight-test campaign. Deliveries of the KAAN to the Turkish Air Force remain scheduled to begin in 2028.

The three prototypes—P0, P1, and P2—feature slightly different designs. The original P0, which completed its maiden flight on 21 February 2024 and a second flight in May of that year, served primarily as a technology demonstrator. P1 and P2 incorporate refinements based on lessons from early flights, including adjustments to the airframe, air intakes, fuselage shaping, and more advanced systems architecture. These differences indicate that the aircraft’s final production configuration has not yet been fully locked in.

Current prototypes are powered by twin General Electric F110 engines, the same family used on Türkiye’s F-16 fleet, while work continues on an indigenous turbofan engine expected to power later production aircraft in the 2030s.

<p>“The test was completed successfully,” Turkish Aerospace Industries stated in connection with the release of the P1 powered-taxi footage.</p>
<p>TUSAŞ General Manager Mehmet Demiroğlu has described the P1 as the point where KAAN enters flight testing in its “real configuration,” distinguishing it from the earlier engineering demonstrator. He has noted that additional prototypes will join the program to increase sortie rates and support the schedule. The company has reiterated that the aim is to fly two KAAN prototypes simultaneously by the end of the year, with the 2028 target for first delivery to the Turkish Air Force remaining in place.</p>

The successful powered taxi tests represent a tangible step forward for one of Türkiye’s most ambitious defense programs. With P1 now in ground testing and P2 progressing in parallel, TUSAŞ is positioning the KAAN to move from single-prototype demonstration toward a multi-aircraft flight-test effort that will underwrite production and eventual operational service.
